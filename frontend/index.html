10 Essential Password Security Rules

Industry Best Practices
01

Never Reuse Passwords

Every online service must have a distinct credential. Credential stuffing turns one third-party compromise into account takeover across all your accounts.

02

Prioritize Length Over Complexity

A 16–20 character passphrase composed of random words yields vastly higher keyspaces than a short 8-character string with awkward symbol substitutions.

03

Avoid Personal Identifiers

Names, birth years, pet names, and hometowns are easily harvestable via Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) and prioritized by custom attack rule engines.

04

Eliminate Keyboard Patterns

Walks like "qwerty", "asdfgh", and "1qaz" are cataloged in automated attacker dictionaries and broken instantly regardless of added punctuation.

05

Deploy a Password Manager

Let an audited password manager generate, encrypt, and autofill 20+ character random credentials so you only ever have to memorize one master passphrase.

06

Always Enable MFA / 2FA

Multi-Factor Authentication (authenticator apps, hardware FIDO2 keys) provides defense-in-depth, neutralizing attacks even if a password is exposed.

07

Never Share Credentials

Avoid sending credentials over email, chat, or spreadsheets. Legitimate IT staff, administrators, and service desks will never request your password.

08

Beware Phishing Simulations & Clones

Always inspect the browser address bar (domain name and TLS certificate) before entering credentials. A strong password cannot defend against being typed into an adversary's phishing portal.

09

Change Compromised Credentials Rapidly

If a service notifies you of a breach or you suspect exposure, immediately rotate the password and revoke active session tokens.

10

Embrace Passkeys & WebAuthn

Where available, migrate toward cryptographic passkeys (FIDO2) which are cryptographically bound to specific domains and immune to credential phishing.
